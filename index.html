About Me

I'm a PhD researcher in the pore network modeling group at KU Leuven, where I work on invasion percolation algorithms — in particular, improving how trapping is handled during fluid flow simulations such as imbibition and drying. My work sits at the intersection of applied mathematics and numerical simulation, and I build most of it in Python using the bwfpnm codebase.

Originally from Iran, I've lived and worked in Leuven for several years now, and I'm drawn to problems where a small change to an algorithm's logic — like how a network handles trapped clusters — can meaningfully change what a simulation predicts.

Research Profile

Research

My work focuses on the pore-scale mechanics of fluid transport in porous materials, and on the algorithms that make those simulations tractable and accurate.

Trapping in Invasion Percolation

Developing and refining trapping algorithms used in invasion percolation, to more accurately capture how fluid clusters become isolated and immobilized during imbibition and drying processes.

Pore Network Modeling (bwfpnm)

Building on the bwfpnm Python codebase to simulate moisture transport through porous materials, connecting network-scale algorithm design to physically meaningful predictions of fluid behavior.

Numerical Methods for Multiphase Flow

Applying numerical simulation and applied mathematics to model multiphase flow phenomena at the pore scale.
